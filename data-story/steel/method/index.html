Steel / Sources and method

How we counted the steel

We reviewed all 13 sites in India where Global Energy Monitor recorded crude-steel furnaces under construction in September 2026. Four projects had enough evidence to put their net additions and industrial sponsors on the map.

The September snapshot

Global Energy Monitor’s Global Iron and Steel Tracker covers plants with at least 500,000 tonnes a year of crude iron or steel capacity. A smaller addition at an eligible plant can appear. The September 2026 v1 release was a limited quarterly update to March’s survey. Here, capacity means the tonnes a facility could make in a year. Actual output can be lower.

We used GEM’s dated public map to fix the Indian plant population, coordinates and capacity by status and furnace type. We also retrieved the public unit records on 25 November 2026, kept steelmaking furnaces and joined their location IDs to the map’s GEM plant IDs. All 42 units matched the frozen totals at every plant and furnace type. The official country table independently gives 37.550 million tonnes a year in construction and 315.506 million announced. The unit API is a later cross-check. The analysis uses the public releases in place of the form-gated workbooks. One announced entry at Srikalahasthi has an unspecified positive capacity. It remains in the source extract and contributes no numeric amount.

Basic oxygen, electric arc, induction, open hearth and unspecified steelmaking capacity qualify. Blast furnaces and direct-reduced iron plants supply iron. Rolling mills process steel that has already been made. Each steelmaking unit contributes once, and a site can have both operating and construction units. GEM treats physical work on the furnace structure as construction.

Following the owners

Each dot begins at GEM’s plant coordinates. The second map assigns the same capacity to countries according to the disclosed equity of the industrial sponsors. We follow a wholly owned project company to its steelmaking parent. A steelmaker that owns its works directly receives the site’s entire allocation. This stopping rule measures who backs the industrial project. A listed steelmaker’s shareholders, lenders, equipment suppliers and fund managers have different roles.

For each sponsor, multiply the included annual capacity by its project stake. An Indian-backed plant has only India-based sponsors. An overseas-backed plant has only sponsors headquartered abroad. A mixed plant has both. Two overseas sponsors make Hazira overseas-backed. A partially disclosed chain leaves the project’s overall class unresolved. In the country view we allocate any identified stakes and retain the residual in grey. The four published projects have complete allocations, with zero mixed or unresolved tonnes.

Points on the country map use Natural Earth’s country label positions. They represent headquarters countries, not city addresses, trade routes or destinations for the steel. The coordinates for Utkal are approximate. Dot area uses the same capacity scale in both map views.

Scope and construction. Four-site coverage is 45.4% of gross tracker capacity. A company’s active integrated project can contain buildings at different stages. At Utkal, July photographs document pellet-plant works, a contractor had the melt-shop piling contract and JSW later described phase one as under construction. We can corroborate the integrated first phase without dating the first converter pile. Removing Utkal brings the Indian share down to 50.2%. With Anakapalle added instead, the overseas share reaches 56.2%. Adding both disputed projects, Anakapalle and Tata’s Neelachal, gives Indian steelmakers 50.6%. These are alternative classifications of the same September projects.

Missing stakes. The main 64.8% Indian share requires no imputation. Even if every unresolved tonne in the separate 13-site gross calculation were assigned overseas, the overseas share of that gross list would reach only 40.8%. The largest documented disagreement concerns when a project qualifies for construction.

Announcements. Adding the announced category increases gross tracker capacity to 353.056 Mt/yr. The Indian parent-country share stays above half with or without announcements. That larger comparison includes projects with uncertain timing, investment-company parents and a joint venture with conflicting stakes. It has no place in the net construction total. The reproducible checks give the precise values under each treatment.

Replacements. We use the documented rise in site capacity when a project also replaces equipment. A like-for-like replacement contributes zero. Hazira’s older proposal to substitute furnaces adds nothing separately to the six-million-tonne expansion. At Ghugus the permit distinguishes steelmaking from the larger finished-products line. Where the outstanding increment could not be isolated, the project stays in the audit.
